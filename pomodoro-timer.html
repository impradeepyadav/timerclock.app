How to use the Pomodoro timer

This free Pomodoro timer follows the classic technique created by Francesco Cirillo: work in focused 25-minute sessions (called Pomodoros), take a 5-minute short break after each, and after completing 4 Pomodoros take a longer 15-minute break before starting the cycle again.

Press Start to begin a focus session. When it ends, a sound plays and the timer automatically switches to the next phase. Use Skip to jump ahead, Reset to restart the current phase, and Settings to change the durations to fit your workflow. Every 4 focus sessions completed is tracked by the dots above the timer.

The Pomodoro cycle

  • Focus (25 min) — one uninterrupted work block. No phone, no email, no distractions.
  • Short break (5 min) — step away from the screen. Stand up, drink water, stretch.
  • Long break (15 min) — earned after 4 focus sessions. Take a real rest before the next set.
  • Repeat — the cycle continues automatically, or manually when you prefer.

Why the Pomodoro Technique works

Short, timed intervals reduce the pressure to "do everything" and instead make it easier to start. The scheduled breaks prevent burnout and keep your attention fresh. Tracking completed sessions gives you a concrete measure of how much deep work you did — which is far more motivating than watching a generic clock.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Pomodoro Technique?

It's a time-management method where you work in focused 25-minute intervals called Pomodoros, take a 5-minute break after each, and after 4 Pomodoros take a longer 15–30 minute break.

How does this Pomodoro timer work?

Press Start to begin a 25-minute focus session. When it ends, a sound plays and the timer switches to a 5-minute short break. After 4 focus sessions, you get a longer 15-minute break, then the cycle repeats.

Can I customize the durations?

Yes. Open Settings to change the focus length, short break, long break, and number of cycles before a long break. Your preferences are saved in your browser.

What do the dots above the timer mean?

They show your progress through the current set of Pomodoros. Each green dot represents a completed focus session; the current one is outlined in green.

Is the Pomodoro timer free?

Yes. It's completely free, requires no signup, and runs directly in your browser on any device.
